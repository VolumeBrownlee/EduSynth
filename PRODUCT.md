# PRODUCT.md — EduSynth

> Context file consumed by `$impeccable` skills. Defines users, brand, tone, and strategic principles.

## Register
`product` — design SERVES the product. Students and lecturers use this to *do work*, not to admire it. Function leads, character supports.

## Users

**Primary: University student (undergraduate, year 2–4).**
- Studying for a specific exam, set by a specific lecturer, on a specific syllabus.
- Mobile-curious but laptop-primary (sessions on a 13–15" screen, library or dormitory).
- Reading-heavy: opens lecture PDFs, takes notes, asks the AI Tutor questions.
- Stake: their grade. Frustration with the tool = abandonment.

**Secondary: Lecturer.**
- Uploads notes, textbooks, and (separately) past papers.
- Reviews cohort analytics to find weak topics.
- Trusts the system with restricted material — needs to *see* the privacy boundary.

## Product Purpose

EduSynth is an AI-native study platform that grounds itself in the lecturer's own course material. It gives students a Socratic AI tutor, AI-generated quizzes and mock challenges, and a free-response Sample Exam Paper modelled on the lecturer's actual exam format. Past papers are restricted-tier and never shown to students directly.

## Brand & Tone

- **Confident, scholarly, with playful gamified moments.** Not a Silicon Valley pitch deck; not a textbook. A serious study tool with a personality.
- **Gamification is intentional, not decorative.** XP titles ("Initiate", "Adept", "Mentor"), the Ready-Score gauge, Study-Handle leaderboard, streaks, "Neural Lab" naming. These are pedagogically motivated (retrieval-practice + spaced-repetition + competition) and must *feel* alive — but never glittery, never childish, never tacky.
- **Tone of UI copy:** direct, helpful, occasionally witty. Never marketing-speak. "Ready-Score 72/100" beats "You're crushing it!".
- **Friendly empty states.** "No past papers yet — your lecturer hasn't uploaded any" beats a 500 error.

## Strategic Principles

1. **Lecturer-grounded.** Every AI surface answers from the lecturer's content, never the open internet. Page references (`[Ref: Page N]`) are non-negotiable.
2. **Privacy by default.** The leaderboard shows Study Handles, not real names — enforced server-side, not just UI-hidden.
3. **Substance over surface.** Beautiful is good, but legible-at-a-glance under reading-fatigue is non-negotiable.
4. **Gamified ≠ noisy.** Restrained motion. Glows and pulses earn their place — they don't fire on every hover.
5. **Two-pane density.** Students compare notes, quizzes, and chat at once. Layouts should respect side-by-side workflows.

## Anti-References (avoid feeling like)

- **Duolingo's cartoon mascot energy.** EduSynth is for grown-up degree-level study.
- **Generic SaaS dashboard cliché.** Big purple gradients, hero numbers, identical icon-cards.
- **Casino-style XP slot-machine.** XP should reward actual learning, not button-mashing.
- **AI slop.** Glassmorphism on every card, gradient text everywhere, indigo `#6366f1`, "AI-powered" copy.

## Inspiration (positive references)

- **Linear** — restraint, density, motion that means something.
- **GitHub** — typography hierarchy under information density.
- **Notion** — friendly, never childish; reading-first; calm.
- **Apple Health rings** — gamification done with quiet visual confidence.
- **Anki** — function-first study tool that built a cult following on substance.
